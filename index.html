Management believes Adjusted EBITDA is a useful supplemental measure of operating performance. Executive Summary — June 2025 Monthly Close

ADP delivered a strong June close, finishing the month ahead of budget on both revenue and net income. Total revenue of $13,130K exceeded plan by $250K (+1.9%), driven by robust client retention in Employer Services and higher-than-anticipated Client Funds Interest income benefiting from the sustained elevated rate environment. Net Income of $1,856K came in $116K above budget, translating to a 14.1% net margin — a 60 basis point improvement versus plan and a 180 basis point improvement over the prior year. EBITDA held at plan at $2,540K. The business enters H2 FY2025 with strong revenue momentum, a healthy pipeline, and disciplined cost management, though targeted attention is required on Service Delivery headcount costs and Global HCM segment reactivation.

Revenue Performance Analysis

Actual $13,130K vs Budget $12,880K · +$250K (+1.9%) · YoY +$670K (+5.4%)
Outperformance Driver — Employer Services

Employer Services revenue of $7,250K exceeded budget by $150K (+2.1%) and grew $270K year-over-year (+3.9%). The outperformance was primarily attributable to higher-than-plan new client activations in the mid-market segment and a 96.2% gross revenue retention rate, 120bps above the 95.0% retention target. Average revenue per client (ARPC) increased approximately 1.4% sequentially, reflecting successful pricing adjustments that took effect May 1.

Upside — Client Funds Interest Income

Client Funds Interest of $620K exceeded budget by $40K (+6.9%) and grew $80K year-over-year (+14.8%). The sustained elevated federal funds rate environment continued to benefit float income on client tax remittance balances. Average float balances increased approximately 3.2% versus the prior month driven by seasonal payroll tax timing, amplified by the June 15 estimated tax deadline. Finance currently projects this line to remain approximately $30–50K above monthly budget through Q3 before normalizing as rate cuts materialize.

Watch Item — Global HCM Segment

Global HCM revenue of $1,840K came in $60K below budget (-3.2%), though it still grew $80K year-over-year (+4.5%). The shortfall reflects delayed go-live timelines for three enterprise implementation projects originally scheduled for June completion that have slipped to July. Revenue recognition on these contracts will be deferred accordingly. The Sales pipeline for Global HCM remains strong at 1.8x quota coverage, and recovery to plan is expected in July–August as the backlog converts.

Profitability & Margin Analysis

Gross Margin 77.4% · EBIT Margin 17.7% · Net Margin 14.1%
Gross Profit — $10,160K (+$140K vs Budget)

Gross profit of $10,160K exceeded budget by $140K, however gross margin compressed 40bps to 77.4% versus the 77.8% budget. The margin compression reflects a $110K cost overrun in Cost of Revenues — principally $60K of above-plan Service Delivery labor attributable to overtime tied to the June payroll processing peak, and $30K of incremental Data Center costs related to capacity provisioning for a new enterprise client onboarded in late May. These costs are expected to normalize in July.

Operating Income — $2,330K (+$10K vs Budget)

Operating Income of $2,330K was essentially in line with budget (+$10K, +0.4%). The gross profit beat was largely offset by $40K of above-plan Sales & Marketing spend attributable to accelerated territory expansion costs in the Southeast region. Partially offsetting this was a $40K R&D underspend driven by delayed vendor invoice submission from a key software development partner — this amount is expected to accrue in July and is not a permanent saving.

Net Income — $1,856K (+$116K vs Budget)

Net Income of $1,856K exceeded budget by $116K, a 6.7% beat. The outperformance was driven by a $5K favorable interest expense result (debt refinancing completed in May locking in a lower rate) combined with $5K of additional other income from sublease income on excess office space. The effective tax rate of 25.0% was consistent with plan. On a year-over-year basis, Net Income grew $321K (+20.9%), reflecting both revenue growth and operating leverage.

Expense Analysis

Total Expenses $5,800K vs Budget $5,680K · +$120K overspend (+2.1%)

Total expenses of $5,800K were $120K above budget for the month. The overspend was concentrated in Cost of Revenues (+$110K) with Operating Expenses broadly on plan (+$10K). The primary drivers are detailed below:

  • Service Delivery (+$60K, +4.0%): June payroll processing peak drove 780 hours of overtime at an average loaded rate of approximately $77/hour. Additionally, three new enterprise client implementations required dedicated onboarding support resources that were not fully anticipated in the original budget. A headcount review is scheduled with the Service Delivery leadership team for July 10 to assess whether additional permanent FTEs or a managed overtime model is more cost-effective for H2.
  • Data Center & Infrastructure (+$30K, +3.2%): Capacity expansion provisioning related to a new 12,000-employee enterprise client (effective May 27) drove incremental cloud compute and storage costs. These costs are expected to stabilize in Q3 as the client's workload normalizes post go-live. The enterprise contract includes a technology surcharge provision that partially offsets this cost beginning in August.
  • Sales & Marketing (+$40K, +3.7%): Accelerated Southeast territory expansion resulted in earlier-than-budgeted hiring of two enterprise account executives (on-board June 3 vs. budget of July 1) and associated onboarding, T&E, and marketing collateral costs. These hires are expected to begin contributing to revenue in Q3 2025.
  • R&D (−$40K, −4.4% favorable): A key software development vendor invoiced $40K of June deliverables in early July, creating a timing difference. This amount will be accrued in the July close. R&D is on track to deliver all planned Q2 product initiatives including the new mobile payroll manager feature and the enhanced ADP DataCloud analytics module.
  • G&A (+$20K, +3.2%): Legal and compliance costs were $20K above budget, attributable to incremental outside counsel fees related to a routine contract review for a new multi-state compliance product offering. This is a one-time item.

Headcount & Labor Analysis

Total HC 12,200 vs Plan 12,150 · +50 over plan · Labor $1,470K vs Budget $1,465K
Headcount Over Plan — Service Delivery

Total headcount of 12,200 ended June 50 FTEs above the plan of 12,150. The over-plan position is entirely attributable to Service Delivery (+120 vs plan), which accelerated hiring to support the enterprise implementation backlog and peak payroll season. Engineering & Technology (−20) and Sales & Marketing (−50) remain modestly below plan due to extended offer-to-start timelines for senior technical roles. G&A is on plan.

Attrition: Annualized voluntary attrition of 15.4% remained within the acceptable 14–16% band. Monthly terminations of 157 were 7 above plan (plan: 150), with the variance concentrated in Service Delivery (8 above plan) reflecting normal mid-year voluntary turnover. Exit interview data indicates compensation competitiveness as the primary driver in 60% of voluntary departures. Total Rewards is conducting a compensation benchmarking exercise for Service Delivery roles, with findings expected by August 1.

Hiring Velocity: 187 new hires were on-boarded in June, 13 below the plan of 200. The shortfall reflects longer-than-expected background check processing times (averaging 18 days vs. the 12-day standard) impacting late-month starts. Talent Acquisition is engaging a secondary background check vendor to address cycle time. The hiring shortfall is expected to reverse in July, with 215 new starts currently in the onboarding pipeline.

Labor Efficiency

Labor cost as a percentage of revenue was 11.2%, 20bps favorable versus the 11.4% budget, reflecting revenue outperformance absorbing the modest labor cost overage. On a year-over-year basis, labor productivity (revenue per FTE) improved approximately 2.1% to $1,076 per month, consistent with the company's stated target of 2–3% annual productivity improvement.

H2 FY2025 Outlook

Forward-looking view · July–December 2025
Full-Year Revenue Guidance
Based on YTD performance ($75,650K) and the current H2 pipeline, Finance projects full-year revenue of $156.5–157.5M, representing 1.5–2.2% above the original full-year budget of $154.6M. This range assumes Global HCM recovers to plan in Q3 and enterprise pipeline conversion rates hold at current levels.
Full-Year Net Income Guidance
Full-year Net Income is projected at $22.1–22.4M, representing a net margin of 14.1–14.2%. This compares to the full-year budget of $20.9M (13.5% margin) and prior year of $18.9M (15.2% of prior year revenue base). Key sensitivity: each 25bps rate cut reduces full-year EBT by approximately $100K.
Key July Close Items
July close will include: (1) $40K R&D vendor accrual catch-up; (2) resolution of three Global HCM deferred go-lives (~$180K revenue); (3) results of Service Delivery HC optimization review; (4) first month of Southeast territory AE productivity contribution. Finance will update FY2025 guidance at the August Board package.
Capital Allocation
No changes to capital allocation framework. Share buyback program remains on track ($42M of the $150M authorization utilized YTD). Q3 dividend of $0.54/share declared June 18, payable July 1. Net leverage ratio of 1.2x EBITDA remains well within the 2.5x covenant threshold.
Actions Required by Finance
1) Complete Service Delivery HC scenario modeling by July 10. 2) Update interest rate sensitivity model following Fed meeting July 30. 3) Prepare revised FY2025 full-year forecast for August 5 CFO review. 4) Close $40K R&D vendor accrual in July.

CFO Commentary — June 2025

Prepared for Board of Directors & Executive Leadership Team · Illustrative sample

June delivered a clean beat on the top and bottom line, and the team should be proud of the execution. Employer Services continues to demonstrate the durability of our core payroll franchise — the 96.2% gross revenue retention is the highest since Q3 2022 and reflects the investments we made in client success over the past 18 months. The Client Funds Interest tailwind has been a meaningful contributor in FY2025 and while we are prudently hedging our rate exposure, I want to ensure the organization does not build this into a permanent baseline assumption as the rate cycle turns.

My principal areas of focus heading into H2 are: (1) Global HCM recovery — I have asked the General Manager to personally sponsor the three deferred enterprise implementations and report weekly until go-live is confirmed; (2) Service Delivery cost discipline — the combination of overtime and above-plan headcount is not sustainable at this rate, and I expect to see a credible plan from operations by July 15; and (3) pipeline-to-revenue conversion — with $4.2M of qualified enterprise ACV in the funnel, Q3 is a critical close quarter and Sales leadership has committed to bi-weekly deal review cadence with me through August.

Overall, ADP is well-positioned for a strong H2 and I am comfortable with the revised full-year guidance range of $156.5–157.5M revenue and $22.1–22.4M net income. We will provide a formal guidance update at the August Board meeting.
